ABC RETAIL PVT. LTD.
BUSINESS METRICS AND KPI DEFINITIONS

Document Code: BMD-001
Department: Data & Analytics
Applicable Area: Business Performance Measurement
Effective Period: January 2025 onwards

1. PURPOSE

ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd. uses standardized business metrics to monitor sales, customers, products, employees, and departments.

The purpose of this document is to establish consistent definitions for commonly used business performance indicators.

All departments should use the approved definitions when discussing company performance.

2. TOTAL ORDERS

Total Orders represents the number of orders recorded by ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd. during a specified period.

Orders are identified using unique order IDs.

Total Orders may include orders with different statuses, including Delivered, Shipped, Pending, Cancelled, and Refunded.

Management should identify the relevant order statuses when interpreting order volume.

3. COMPLETED ORDERS

A Completed Order is an order with the status Delivered.

Delivered indicates that the customer's order has successfully reached the customer.

Completed order analysis is useful for evaluating successfully fulfilled purchases.

4. CANCELLED ORDERS

Cancelled Orders represent orders with the status Cancelled.

Cancelled orders should be analyzed separately from completed orders.

Cancellation analysis may help management understand customer and operational issues.

5. REFUNDED ORDERS

Refunded Orders represent orders with the status Refunded.

Refunded orders should be monitored separately from normal completed sales.

Refund activity can be reviewed by product, category, customer segment, city, and time period.

6. SALES REVENUE

Sales revenue represents the value associated with completed customer purchases according to company sales reporting rules.

Delivered orders represent completed purchases.

Management should distinguish completed sales from pending, cancelled, and refunded transactions when evaluating final business performance.

7. AVERAGE ORDER VALUE

Average Order Value, commonly referred to as AOV, represents the average value of a completed customer order.

The standard company calculation is:

AOV = Total Completed Sales / Number of Completed Orders

AOV should be interpreted alongside order volume because an increase in AOV does not necessarily mean that the total number of customers or orders has increased.

8. UNITS SOLD

Units Sold represents the total quantity of products included in customer orders.

Quantity is recorded in the order_items records.

Units sold can be evaluated by:

* Product
* Category
* Customer
* Customer segment
* City
* State
* Month
* Year

9. PRODUCT SALES

Product sales represent sales associated with individual products.

Product analysis may include:

* Units sold
* Number of orders
* Selling price
* Product cost
* Sales contribution
* Gross profit

Product IDs provide the connection between products and order items.

10. CATEGORY SALES

Category sales represent the sales contribution of products belonging to a specific category.

Category performance may be compared according to:

* Sales
* Units sold
* Number of orders
* Average product price
* Product cost
* Profit contribution

Category comparisons should consider differences in product count and transaction volume.

11. GROSS PROFIT

Gross profit represents the difference between product sales value and the associated product cost.

At the product level, basic unit gross profit can be represented as:

Gross Profit per Unit = Price - Cost

For multiple units:

Gross Profit = Sales Value - Product Cost Value

Gross profit does not represent final company profit because other operating expenses may not be included.

12. GROSS MARGIN

Gross Margin represents gross profit as a percentage of sales.

The standard calculation is:

Gross Margin % = (Gross Profit / Sales) × 100

Management may review gross margin by product and category.

A product with high sales does not necessarily have the highest gross margin.

13. CUSTOMER COUNT

Customer Count represents the number of customers recorded in the customer master.

Each customer should have a unique customer ID.

Customer count may be analyzed by:

* Segment
* City
* State
* Signup period

14. CUSTOMER SEGMENT DISTRIBUTION

The company maintains five customer segments:

Premium
Regular
Occasional
New
At Risk

Management may compare the number of customers in each segment.

Segment distribution provides an overview of the composition of the company's customer base.

15. NEW CUSTOMER COUNT

New Customer Count represents customers classified as New according to the company's customer segmentation rules.

New customer activity should be considered separately from total customer count.

Management may compare new customer registrations with subsequent purchasing activity.

16. ACTIVE CUSTOMER

An active customer is a customer who demonstrates relevant purchasing activity during the selected reporting period.

The exact reporting definition should be specified for each business report.

Customer activity may be evaluated using order records and completed purchases.

17. REPEAT CUSTOMER

A repeat customer is a customer who has made more than one qualifying purchase according to the reporting period and business definition being used.

Repeat customer analysis helps management understand continued customer engagement.

18. SALES GROWTH

Sales Growth represents the change in sales between two comparable periods.

The standard percentage-change calculation is:

Growth % = ((Current Period Sales - Previous Period Sales) / Previous Period Sales) × 100

Growth should be compared between equivalent periods where possible.

For example, monthly sales may be compared with the previous month or the same month in the previous year.

19. MONTH-OVER-MONTH GROWTH

Month-over-Month growth compares a month's performance with the immediately preceding month.

Management may use MoM growth to identify recent changes in sales activity.

20. YEAR-OVER-YEAR GROWTH

Year-over-Year growth compares a period with the corresponding period from the previous year.

YoY comparisons are useful when seasonal purchasing patterns may affect monthly results.

21. ORDER STATUS DISTRIBUTION

Order status distribution shows the number or percentage of orders in each status.

The company-approved statuses are:

* Delivered
* Shipped
* Pending
* Cancelled
* Refunded

Status distribution helps management understand the current order lifecycle.

22. CANCELLATION RATE

Cancellation Rate measures cancelled orders relative to the selected order population.

A common calculation is:

Cancellation Rate = Cancelled Orders / Total Orders × 100

The reporting period and denominator should always be clearly specified.

23. REFUND RATE

Refund Rate measures refunded orders relative to the selected order population.

A common calculation is:

Refund Rate = Refunded Orders / Total Orders × 100

Management should interpret refund rate together with product category, order volume, and customer behavior.

24. EMPLOYEE HEADCOUNT

Employee Headcount represents the number of employees recorded in the employee master.

Employees may be analyzed by:

* Department
* Job role
* City
* Hire year

25. AVERAGE SALARY

Average Salary represents the arithmetic mean salary of employees in the selected population.

Average salary can be reviewed by:

* Department
* Job role
* City

Individual salary information should remain confidential.

26. DEPARTMENT BUDGET

Each company department has an annual budget.

The company's departments are:

Sales
Data & Analytics
Human Resources
Finance
Operations

Management may compare department-level employee salary expenditure with annual departmental budgets.

27. DEPARTMENT BUDGET UTILIZATION

Budget utilization compares relevant department expenditure with the approved annual department budget.

Management should clearly identify which expenses are included before interpreting budget utilization.

Employee salary expenditure may be one component of department spending but should not automatically be treated as the entire department expenditure.

28. EMPLOYEE TENURE

Employee tenure represents the length of time an employee has been employed by the company.

Hire Date is used as the starting point for tenure calculations.

Management may review tenure by department and job role.

29. KPI COMPARISONS

Business metrics should be compared using consistent definitions.

When comparing periods, management should ensure:

* Same metric definition
* Comparable time periods
* Consistent order-status rules
* Consistent customer definitions
* Consistent product definitions

30. MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLE

ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd. uses standardized metrics to create consistent business reporting.

Metrics should be clearly defined before being used for management decisions.

Sales metrics should be interpreted alongside customer, product, and operational information.

Employee metrics should be interpreted alongside department structure and budgets.

Consistent definitions help different departments communicate using the same understanding of company performance.
