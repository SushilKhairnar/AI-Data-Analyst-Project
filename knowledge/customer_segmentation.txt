ABC RETAIL PVT. LTD.
CUSTOMER SEGMENTATION POLICY

Document Code: CSP-001
Department: Sales and Customer Management
Applicable Area: Customer Classification and Relationship Management
Effective Period: January 2025 onwards

1. PURPOSE

ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd. uses customer segmentation to organize customers according to their relationship with the company and their purchasing behavior.

The customer master contains a unique customer ID, customer name, city, state, signup date, and customer segment.

Customer segmentation helps the Sales and Customer Management teams understand different groups of customers and apply appropriate business strategies.

The company currently maintains the following customer segments:

* Premium
* Regular
* Occasional
* New
* At Risk

2. CUSTOMER MASTER

Every customer must have a unique customer ID.

Customer records contain:

Customer ID
Customer Name
City
State
Signup Date
Customer Segment

The customer ID is the primary identifier used by the company to distinguish one customer from another.

Customer information must be kept accurate and updated when valid changes are reported.

3. PREMIUM CUSTOMERS

Premium customers represent customers with a strong and valuable relationship with ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd.

These customers generally demonstrate higher purchasing activity, stronger engagement, or greater business value.

Premium customers should receive appropriate attention from the Sales and Customer Service teams.

The company may review Premium customers for:

* Purchase frequency
* Order activity
* Sales contribution
* Product preferences
* Geographic distribution
* Customer retention
* Recent purchasing activity

Premium customers should be monitored to maintain a positive long-term relationship.

Sales representatives should understand the purchasing patterns of Premium customers and ensure that important service issues are addressed promptly.

4. REGULAR CUSTOMERS

Regular customers maintain an ongoing relationship with the company.

They purchase products periodically and represent an important part of the company's recurring customer base.

Regular customers should be monitored for purchasing consistency.

The Sales Department may review:

* Number of orders
* Purchase frequency
* Product categories purchased
* Total purchase activity
* Changes in purchasing behavior
* Geographic distribution

The objective is to maintain regular customer activity and encourage continued engagement.

5. OCCASIONAL CUSTOMERS

Occasional customers purchase products less frequently.

Their purchasing activity may occur at irregular intervals.

The company should monitor Occasional customers to understand whether their low purchasing frequency is related to customer preferences, product demand, pricing, or other business factors.

Sales teams may review previous purchases and product preferences when communicating with these customers.

Occasional customers should not automatically be treated as inactive customers because an individual customer's purchasing cycle may vary.

6. NEW CUSTOMERS

New customers are customers who have recently joined ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd.

The signup date recorded in the customer master is used to understand the customer's relationship period with the company.

New customers require particular attention during their initial relationship.

The Sales and Customer Service teams should ensure that new customers understand:

* Available products
* Ordering procedures
* Customer support channels
* Delivery procedures
* Cancellation procedures
* Refund procedures

The company should monitor whether new customers complete their first purchase and whether they continue purchasing after their initial interaction.

7. AT RISK CUSTOMERS

At Risk customers are customers whose relationship with the company requires additional attention because their purchasing activity has declined or shows signs of reduced engagement.

At Risk customers should be reviewed by the Sales and Customer Management teams.

The company may examine:

* Previous order activity
* Recent order activity
* Time since last purchase
* Previously purchased categories
* Customer location
* Historical purchasing frequency

An At Risk classification should be treated as an indicator for customer review rather than an automatic conclusion that the customer has permanently stopped purchasing.

8. CUSTOMER SEGMENT REVIEW

Customer segments should be reviewed periodically.

The company may compare segments using:

* Customer count
* Order count
* Completed purchases
* Total sales
* Average order value
* Purchase frequency
* Product category preference
* Geographic distribution

Segment reviews help management understand the composition of the customer base.

9. CUSTOMER GEOGRAPHY

Customer location consists of city and state information.

Geographic analysis may be used to understand the distribution of customer segments.

For example, management may review how many Premium, Regular, New, Occasional, or At Risk customers are located in particular cities or states.

Geographic information should be used carefully because customer location alone does not determine customer value.

10. CUSTOMER LIFECYCLE

ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd. recognizes that customers move through different stages of their relationship with the company.

A customer may initially enter as a New customer.

With continued purchasing activity, the customer may become a Regular or Premium customer.

A customer with lower purchasing activity may become Occasional or At Risk depending on the customer's circumstances and company classification rules.

Customer lifecycle reviews should consider actual customer activity rather than relying on a single indicator.

11. CUSTOMER PURCHASING BEHAVIOR

The company reviews purchasing behavior using order information.

Important customer-level indicators include:

* Number of orders
* Purchase frequency
* Products purchased
* Product categories purchased
* Sales contribution
* Recent purchasing activity
* Order status history

Completed purchases provide important information about actual customer buying behavior.

Cancelled and refunded orders should be considered separately when reviewing customer activity.

12. CUSTOMER RETENTION

Customer retention is an important objective of customer management.

The Sales Department should monitor whether customers continue purchasing from the company after their first order.

Retention reviews may compare customers who purchased during different periods.

The company should pay attention to customers showing reduced activity and determine whether additional communication or service support is appropriate.

13. CUSTOMER ACQUISITION

New customer acquisition is important for maintaining the company's customer base.

The customer signup date provides information about when customers entered the company's database.

Management may review new customer registrations over time and compare new customer acquisition with subsequent purchasing activity.

A customer registration should not automatically be considered a successful sale because registration and purchasing are separate business events.

14. CUSTOMER VALUE

Customer value may be evaluated using purchasing activity, order frequency, completed sales, and product demand.

The company should avoid evaluating customer value using only one metric.

For example, a customer with a large number of orders may have a different purchasing pattern from a customer with fewer but higher-value purchases.

Customer value analysis should consider the complete available customer history.

15. CUSTOMER SERVICE

Customer service teams should provide appropriate support to all customer segments.

Premium customers may require priority attention based on approved company service procedures.

New customers should receive clear guidance regarding company processes.

At Risk customers may require additional relationship management.

Occasional and Regular customers should continue receiving appropriate service based on their needs.

Customer segmentation should never be used as a reason to deny basic customer support.

16. CUSTOMER DATA ACCURACY

Customer IDs must be unique.

Customer names should be recorded accurately.

City and state information should be maintained consistently.

Signup dates must represent valid dates.

Customer segment values must use approved company classifications.

Duplicate customer records should be investigated.

Incorrect customer information should be corrected by authorized personnel.

17. CUSTOMER PRIVACY

Customer information is company business data and must be handled responsibly.

Customer names, locations, signup information, and purchasing activity should only be accessed for legitimate business purposes.

Employees should not disclose customer information outside approved business processes.

Customer information should be protected from unauthorized access.

18. SEGMENT MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLES

The objective of segmentation is to help ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd. understand its customers and improve customer relationships.

Premium customers should be retained through strong service and relationship management.

Regular customers should be encouraged to maintain consistent purchasing activity.

Occasional customers should be understood rather than automatically classified as inactive.

New customers should receive appropriate onboarding and support.

At Risk customers should be reviewed for declining activity and possible retention opportunities.

19. MANAGEMENT REVIEW

Customer segmentation reports should be reviewed periodically by Sales and Customer Management leadership.

Management should consider customer counts, purchasing behavior, geographic distribution, product preferences, and changes over time.

Segment classifications should remain consistent across company reports.

The customer segmentation system should support better understanding of customer relationships while maintaining accuracy, fairness, and responsible handling of customer information.
