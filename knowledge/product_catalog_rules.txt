ABC RETAIL PVT. LTD.
PRODUCT CATALOG RULES

Document Code: PCR-001
Department: Product Management and Sales
Applicable Area: Product Master Data and Product Sales Management
Effective Period: January 2025 onwards

1. PURPOSE

The Product Catalog Rules establish the standards for maintaining products sold by ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd.

The product catalog is the company's central record of products available for sales operations.

Every product is identified using a unique product ID.

The product catalog contains:

* Product ID
* Product Name
* Category
* Price
* Cost

Product information must remain accurate because it is used by Sales, Finance, Operations, Customer Service, and Management.

2. PRODUCT IDENTIFICATION

Each product must have a unique product ID.

The product ID is the official identifier used throughout the company's sales records.

When a product is included in an order, the order item must reference the corresponding product ID.

Employees should not create alternative identifiers for existing products.

Product IDs must remain consistent across company records.

3. PRODUCT NAME

Every product must have a clear and recognizable product name.

Product names should allow Sales and Customer Service employees to identify products easily.

Product names should be consistent across reports and business communications.

Duplicate product names should be investigated because they can create confusion in sales and product reporting.

4. PRODUCT CATEGORY

Every product belongs to a product category.

Categories allow the company to organize its product portfolio and analyze customer demand.

Category-level reviews may include:

* Number of products
* Number of orders
* Units sold
* Sales contribution
* Product cost
* Profit contribution
* Customer demand

Products should be assigned to the appropriate approved category.

5. PRODUCT PRICE

The product price represents the standard selling price maintained by ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd.

Sales employees must use the approved price associated with the product.

Product prices should be reviewed periodically by the responsible business team.

Any approved price change should be reflected in the official product catalog.

Employees should not independently modify product prices without authorization.

6. PRODUCT COST

The product cost represents the company's recorded cost associated with the product.

Cost information is important for evaluating product profitability.

Management may compare product price and cost to understand the financial contribution of different products.

Cost information should be maintained carefully because incorrect cost values can affect profitability analysis.

7. PRODUCT PROFITABILITY

Product profitability may be evaluated by comparing the product selling price with its recorded cost.

At the individual product level, the difference between price and cost represents the basic unit-level gross profit before considering other business expenses.

Management may review:

* Product price
* Product cost
* Unit profit
* Units sold
* Total product sales
* Total product cost
* Gross profit contribution

Products with strong sales volume and strong margins may have a different business impact from products with high sales volume but lower margins.

8. PRODUCT SALES PERFORMANCE

The Sales and Product Management teams should regularly review product performance.

Important indicators include:

* Number of units sold
* Number of orders
* Product sales value
* Product category
* Customer segments purchasing the product
* Geographic demand
* Profit contribution

Product performance should be reviewed over appropriate periods to identify changes in customer demand.

9. PRODUCT CATEGORY PERFORMANCE

Category performance provides management with a broader view of the company's product portfolio.

A category containing many products may generate a different level of sales than a smaller category.

Management may compare categories based on:

* Sales value
* Units sold
* Number of orders
* Average product price
* Average product cost
* Profit contribution
* Customer demand

Category performance should be reviewed regularly.

10. PRODUCT DEMAND

Customer order information provides evidence of product demand.

When customers purchase a product, the corresponding order item records the product ID and quantity.

Higher quantities indicate greater unit demand within the available order records.

Product demand should be evaluated across time periods rather than using a single order.

The company may review monthly, quarterly, and yearly product demand.

11. PRODUCT QUANTITY

Order items record the quantity of each product purchased.

Quantity represents the number of units included in the customer's order.

Product quantity must be recorded accurately.

Incorrect quantity values can affect sales reports, product demand analysis, and profitability calculations.

Sales employees should verify quantity before completing an order.

12. PRODUCT AND CUSTOMER SEGMENTS

Different customer segments may demonstrate different product preferences.

ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd. may review product purchases across:

* Premium customers
* Regular customers
* Occasional customers
* New customers
* At Risk customers

The company may identify products that are frequently purchased by particular customer segments.

Such information can support product planning and customer relationship activities.

13. PRODUCT AND GEOGRAPHIC DEMAND

Customer city and state information can be used to understand where different products are purchased.

Management may review product demand across geographic areas.

For example, a product may have strong demand in one city and lower demand in another.

Geographic product analysis can help the company understand differences in customer preferences.

14. PRODUCT PORTFOLIO MANAGEMENT

ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd. should maintain a balanced product portfolio.

Product managers should periodically review products according to their sales activity, customer demand, category, price, cost, and profitability.

Products with declining sales should be reviewed to understand the underlying business reasons.

Products with increasing demand should be monitored to ensure that the company can continue supporting customer requirements.

15. PRODUCT DATA QUALITY

The following rules apply to product master data:

Product IDs must be unique.

Product names must be meaningful.

Every product should have an appropriate category.

Prices must be valid business values.

Costs must be valid business values.

Product records should not contain unnecessary duplicates.

Changes to product information should be authorized.

Any inconsistency between product records and sales records should be investigated.

16. PRODUCT CHANGES

Product information may change during the normal operation of the business.

Approved changes may include:

* Product name changes
* Category changes
* Price changes
* Cost updates

Changes should be documented and reviewed by the responsible department.

Historical business reports should be interpreted carefully when product information has changed.

17. PRODUCT REPORTING

Product reports should provide management with a clear understanding of product performance.

Regular product reports may include:

* Top-selling products
* Products with highest unit demand
* Products with highest sales contribution
* Category performance
* Product profitability
* Customer segment preferences
* Geographic product demand

Reports should use consistent product identifiers.

18. SALES AND PRODUCT TEAMS

The Product Management team is responsible for maintaining product information.

The Sales team uses product information during customer interactions and order processing.

The Finance team may use product price and cost information for profitability analysis.

Operations teams may use product information for order fulfillment and business planning.

All departments should use the official product catalog.

19. PRODUCT PRICING REVIEW

Product prices should be reviewed when business conditions require changes.

Price reviews may consider product cost, customer demand, market conditions, product category, and business objectives.

Only authorized employees should approve pricing changes.

Sales personnel should not promise unauthorized prices to customers.

20. MANAGEMENT PRINCIPLE

The product catalog is a core business asset of ABC Retail Pvt. Ltd.

Accurate product IDs, names, categories, prices, and costs are essential for reliable sales operations.

Product management should focus on maintaining accurate product information, understanding customer demand, monitoring profitability, and ensuring that the company's product portfolio continues to support customer and business requirements.
